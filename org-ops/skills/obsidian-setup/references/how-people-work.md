# How people actually work in Obsidian

There is no org-approved method, and imposing one is how this gets abandoned in a
fortnight. Ask which of these sounds like them, set that up, and stop.

Most people are the third one. That is fine and it is not a lesser answer.

---

## Ask two questions first

1. **When you take a note, is it about a day or about a thing?** A day means meetings,
   calls, and what happened. A thing means a project, an account, a topic.
2. **Do you go back and reread your notes, or is writing them the point?**

Someone who says "about a day" and "I reread them" wants daily notes. Someone who says
"about a thing" wants folders. Someone who says "writing them is the point, I never look
again" wants search and nothing else, and setting up templates for them is wasted effort.

---

## The three patterns

### Daily notes

One note per day. Meetings, calls, and thoughts land in today's note and get sorted
later, or never.

**Set up:** enable the **Daily notes** core plugin, set a folder (`Notes/Daily/`), give
them a small template with the headings they actually use.

**Fits** people who live in their calendar, take a lot of meetings, and think
chronologically. Sales, membership, and exec assistants often land here.

**Fails** when someone forgets for a week and then feels behind. If they seem likely to
treat an empty daily note as a chore, do not set this up.

### A folder per thing

One folder per project, account, or program. Notes go where the work is.

**Set up:** nothing to enable. Make folders under `Work/` matching what they already do,
and use the same names as their existing shared drive, SharePoint, or Google Drive
folders so there is one mental model
rather than two.

**Fits** people with a stable set of ongoing responsibilities. Most program and research
staff.

**Fails** when a note does not obviously belong anywhere, and they stall deciding. Tell
them to drop it in `Notes/` and let search find it.

### Just write and search

No structure at all. Everything in one folder. Find it with `Cmd-Shift-F`.

**Set up:** nothing. Genuinely nothing.

**Fits** most people, and it is the honest default. Obsidian's search is fast enough that
filing is optional, and people who file only because they were told to stop doing it
within a month.

**Fails** only when the vault gets very large and titles are unhelpful. Fix that then,
not now.

---

## What to teach, in order

Four things on day one, and nothing else. More than four and none of them stick.

1. **`Cmd-Shift-F` searches everything**, every note at once, including the words
   inside them.
2. **`Cmd-O` jumps to any note by name.** The fastest thing in the app.
3. **Type `[[` to link.** Then show the **backlink** appearing on the other note. This is
   the one idea worth understanding, because it is what a folder cannot do.
4. **`Cmd-N` makes a note.** Where it lands matters less than they think.

Leave these alone until asked: plugins, canvas, graph view beyond ten seconds, tags
versus folders, and any published methodology. Every one of them is a way to spend an
afternoon and produce nothing.

## Tags or folders

Whichever they already think in. Do not run the argument. If pressed: folders for where
something lives, tags for a state that cuts across folders, like `#waiting` or
`#needs-review`. Both are optional.

## Their vault is theirs

Worth saying explicitly, because people assume otherwise when a work tool touches their
machine. The vault is local. It is not synced to the org, nobody else reads it, and there is
no admin view of it. Personal notes in there are their business.

Nothing travels. There is no company vault to publish into and no step in this skill
that copies their notes anywhere. If a request starts to sound like moving personal notes
into a shared company location, that is the wrong shape. See [[import-by-tool]].
